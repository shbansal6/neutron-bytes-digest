Neutron Bytes | Weekly Nuclear News Digest

Coverage: Monday 12 October–Sunday 18 October 2026

Executive summary

Kairos Power reported $643 million of combined private and federal financing for its Hermes 2 demonstration, while the NRC renewed the AP1000 design certification through 2066. Fuel-cycle activity included Bluecore’s purchase of 4.95%-enriched uranium, Newcleo’s €10 million MOX development center and an 80/20 proposed South African enrichment venture. Poland’s SGE–ZE PAK venture outlined up to 1,800 MWe of BWRX-300 capacity; Germany launched a fusion program with up to €2.5 billion in conditional public support. These announcements span different levels of maturity: financing, design certification, site development and industrial R&D are not interchangeable with commercial operation.

Published during the week: Two Neutron Bytes posts (October 15 and 16); 11 substantive items from those posts, including one industry-analysis article.

Capital & financing

Kairos secures $643M for Hermes 2 demonstration reactor — Oct 16, 2026

Neutron Bytes reported $643 million of combined financing for Kairos Power’s Hermes 2 program: $368 million from investors and $275 million from DOE. Hermes 2 is a planned 50 MWe grid-connected demonstration in Tennessee, aligned with the Google/Kairos/TVA offtake arrangement. The report separately describes an up-to-$100 million Samsung C&T partnership; that figure should not automatically be added to the $643 million total.

Sources: Neutron Bytes · Kairos / Google 500 MW agreement (background)

Construction & licensing

NRC updates AP1000 design certification through 2066 — Oct 16, 2026

The NRC updated and renewed Westinghouse AP1000 design certification, incorporating lessons from Vogtle Units 3 and 4 and extending the certification through 2066. This may reduce repetitive design review for future units, but site-specific approvals and construction permits remain necessary.

Sources: Neutron Bytes · NRC — AP1000 design certification

V.C. Summer completion faces fresh AP1000 licensing and site-condition reviews — Oct 16, 2026

Brookfield and Santee Cooper are exploring completion of two unfinished AP1000 units (2 × 1,150 MWe) in South Carolina. Neutron Bytes says a final investment decision is expected by end-2028, not yet approved. Because the NRC terminated the project’s combined licenses in March 2019, updated design certification alone does not permit construction to resume. A new licensing application would need to address the current safety basis and environmental information, while engineers assess concrete, steel, prefabricated modules and other site assets exposed since construction stopped in 2017. The article discusses possible Part 50 and Part 52 pathways but does not report that either has been selected. This is a project-execution and licensing assessment, not a newly issued project approval.

Sources: Neutron Bytes · NRC V.C. Summer licensing record

SGE and ZE PAK propose six BWRX-300 units at Poland’s Pątnów coal site — Oct 16, 2026

Under the proposed joint venture, ZE PAK would contribute rights to land at its existing coal-fired generation site; it may retain a minority interest or subsequently exit. The project could deploy six 300 MWe BWRX-300 units, for up to 1,800 MWe, but no final investment decision, construction start or financing package was reported. Neutron Bytes also notes SGE’s broader Polish pipeline, with decisions in principle covering 26 BWRX-300 units, and a separate UK proposal for 14 units. Those pipeline figures should not be confused with funded construction orders.

Sources: Neutron Bytes · World Nuclear News — Pątnów joint venture

Fuel & supply chain

Bluecore buys LEU for proposed 10 MWe floating reactor — Oct 16, 2026

Bluecore Energy reported acquiring uranium hexafluoride enriched to 4.95% U-235 while beginning NRC / Coast Guard engagement on a proposed 10 MWe barge-based PWR. It is a tangible fuel-procurement and regulatory step, not a licensed maritime nuclear station; marine classification and siting remain material hurdles.

Sources: Neutron Bytes · Splash247 — Bluecore nuclear barge development · NRC — Bluecore regulatory engagement

Newcleo opens €10M MOX fuel development center in France — Oct 16, 2026

The FASTER center near Marcoule supports engineering validation, fuel-fabrication mock-ups and workforce training for a team of roughly 100 specialists. Its seven operating facilities address activities including powder dosing, glovebox handling, pellet inspection and fuel-pin assembly. The opening is separate from Newcleo’s proposed commercial MOX fabrication plant near Nogent-sur-Seine: Neutron Bytes cites a possible €1.8 billion initial investment for three lines and a further €3.2 billion for two more lines by 2040. The future MOX plant remains subject to approvals and investment decisions; the new €10 million facility is not itself commercial fuel-production capacity.

Sources: Neutron Bytes · World Nuclear News — Newcleo FASTER center

ASP Isotopes affiliate and Necsa outline South African uranium enrichment venture — Oct 16, 2026

Quantum Leap Energy, associated with U.S.-listed ASP Isotopes, would lead the venture and arrange financing; state-owned Necsa would provide site access and nuclear-regulatory and safeguards support. Neutron Bytes identifies TerraPower as a prospective purchaser under previously arranged supply agreements. The proposed facility has not entered commercial production, and project cost, nameplate enrichment capacity and commissioning schedule were not disclosed. ASP Isotopes cautioned that its enrichment technology has not yet been applied to uranium-235, making technical validation, financing and regulatory approvals material outstanding milestones.

Sources: Neutron Bytes · Business Insider Africa — Pelindaba venture

Policy & international

DOE and ABS sign framework for commercial nuclear power at sea — Oct 16, 2026

The MOU calls for feasibility assessments and development of technical and industrial capabilities for nuclear energy in maritime applications, including floating nuclear power, nuclear-powered vessels, ports and shipyards. Neutron Bytes places it alongside 2026 Maritime Administration initiatives at the ports of Long Beach and Corpus Christi. This is a cooperation framework—not a licensed reactor, funded construction contract or deployment order. Commercial projects will still need to resolve regulatory jurisdiction, vessel classification, safeguards and operating requirements.

Sources: Neutron Bytes · NucNet — DOE / ABS MOU

Guest analysis: advanced-reactor pipeline faces licensing, capital and operating hurdles — Oct 15, 2026

Sarah Gibboney’s Neutron Bytes guest column groups deployment risk into four areas: regulatory credibility, capital and supply-chain readiness, organizational maturity, and operations and maintenance. Her proprietary tracker lists 125 developer companies and 178 distinct reactor designs; these are author-reported counts, not independently audited industry totals. She also notes the NRC’s April 2026 commercial-use criterion for certain Section 104 research licenses: energy sales exceeding 50% of annual operating costs trigger Section 103 commercial-license treatment. This is industry analysis published October 15, not an announcement that 178 reactors are under construction or that a specific project reached commercial operation.

Sources: Neutron Bytes · NRC — regulatory modernization background

Fusion & research

DOE approves cost-shared next phase for molten chloride reactor experiment — Oct 16, 2026

The MCRE is a fast-spectrum molten chloride salt reactor experiment intended to produce engineering and operating data for future advanced-reactor designs. The project targets criticality and fuel-salt performance research, not grid electricity sales or a marine power deployment. Neutron Bytes describes the experiment as approximately 300 kW thermal and says the new funding supports final-stage development; the article does not specify the amount of this award. Future closed-fuel-cycle, spent-fuel and maritime uses are prospective technology applications, not outcomes demonstrated by MCRE.

Sources: Neutron Bytes · ANS Nuclear Newswire — DOE cost-shared award

Germany establishes milestone-based fusion program with up to €2.5B of public support — Oct 16, 2026

The program is administered by SPRIND on behalf of the federal research ministry and is intended to mobilize additional private capital for fusion technology demonstrations. It complements Germany’s Fusion 2040 initiative and national research hubs at Biblis, Karlsruhe and Garching. The €2.5 billion is a prospective program ceiling, not funding already disbursed to one company. Neutron Bytes references RWE-backed Focused Energy and Proxima Fusion as examples of the emerging industrial pipeline, with a commercial-scale fusion plant targeted in the 2040s rather than currently operating.

Sources: Neutron Bytes · NucNet — Germany fusion milestone program
